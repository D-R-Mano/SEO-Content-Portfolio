## The communication problem

Electrical earthing products involve technical specifications, installation requirements, and safety considerations. The people reading about them have different responsibilities. An engineer needs technical detail; a buyer needs information relevant to evaluation; a customer may first need a plain-language explanation of the product's purpose.

My work at Ashlok covered technical and marketing content. The challenge was to make product information understandable across those audiences while keeping the messaging consistent.

## My contribution

I developed website and product copy, including metadata and SEO-focused descriptions. I also worked on email call-outs, social announcements, product newsletters, and presentations for international clients and industry events. My technical writing included installation and assembly information derived from product specifications and diagrams.

These formats required different levels of detail. A social announcement had to communicate the essential update quickly. A product page needed space to explain the offering. Installation content needed explicit instructions grounded in technical source material.

## How I approached the work

**Adapt the explanation to the audience.** I translated specifications into language relevant to the reader's context, instead of using the same wording across every channel.

**Connect the message across formats.** Website content, email copy, announcements, and presentations needed a consistent account of the product even when the length and emphasis changed.

**Keep technical meaning intact.** Making copy accessible did not remove the need to work from specifications and installation references. This connection between explanation and technical accuracy remains central to my work.
