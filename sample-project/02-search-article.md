# Duplicate support tickets: what to check first

*Independent writing sample for the fictional RelayDesk product.*

Two tickets from the same customer do not always describe the same problem. Before you combine them, read both conversations and check what the customer is asking your team to do.

If the tickets describe one issue, keeping the conversation together can make it easier for the team to follow the history. If they describe separate issues, keeping them separate preserves a clear record of each request.

## Start with the conversation

Compare the customer, the request, and the information already shared. Similar subject lines alone are not enough to establish that two tickets are duplicates.

For example, “I can't access my account” and “Please update my billing address” may come from the same person but need different actions. Two submissions describing the same access problem may be candidates for a merge after the team reviews them.

## Check what your support tool allows

In RelayDesk, an admin or team lead can merge two open tickets belonging to the same customer. An agent cannot perform the merge. Closed tickets and tickets belonging to different customers are ineligible. These are rules of this fictional product; other support tools may behave differently.

Before merging, decide which ticket should remain active. RelayDesk moves the source ticket's messages into the destination conversation in chronological order. It closes the source and adds a reference to the destination. The destination retains its owner and status.

**A merge cannot be undone in RelayDesk.** Check both ticket IDs and the destination before confirming.

[Follow the step-by-step merge guide](03-help-guide.md).

## If Merge is unavailable

Check three things:

- Are you signed in as an admin or team lead?
- Are both tickets open?
- Do they belong to the same customer?

If all three conditions are met and the option is still unavailable, contact support with the two ticket IDs and the time you tried. Do not include passwords or copy customer messages into an unapproved channel.

## Merging does not prevent another submission

A merge organizes tickets that already exist. It does not change routing or prevent a customer from submitting another request.

If repeated tickets continue to appear, collect a few ticket IDs and their timestamps for your support team to investigate. Avoid assuming a cause from the subject line alone. The available information may be insufficient to distinguish repeated customer submissions from another problem.

## Choose the next step

If both tickets concern one issue and meet the product rules, [review the merge procedure](03-help-guide.md). If the issues differ, keep the records separate. If you cannot establish the cause of repeated tickets, ask support to investigate rather than treating a merge as a permanent fix.
